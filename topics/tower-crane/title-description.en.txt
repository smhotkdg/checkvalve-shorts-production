Description
As a building rises, its tower crane has to rise with it.
With no bigger crane to help, the crane lifts its own top with a climbing cage and a hydraulic jack, then slides a new mast section into the gap, one at a time.
Balancing with the trolley, the two-stroke hydraulic climb held by pawls, and sliding in and bolting the new section, shown in 3D animation.

Conceptual animation for explanation; it does not reproduce a specific crane model's procedure or figures.
Core structure: Blender / some shots: Higgsfield / narration: Typecast (Hugh)

Final English narration
A tower crane lifts itself.
Sounds insane, right?
So how does a new section fit underneath it?
Buildings rise, the crane must follow, and no bigger crane can help.
So a cage wraps the top, and a new section waits on a rail.
Now the real problem: the top must be unbolted from the mast.
To avoid tipping, the trolley balances it first.
Bolts out, a hydraulic jack braces on the mast and lifts the whole top.
Too far for one stroke, so pawls hold it and it climbs twice.
The new section slides into the gap, bolts on, and the top locks down.
Lifting itself, it grows with the building, one section at a time.
