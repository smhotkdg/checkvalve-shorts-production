Leave the fridge open to cool your room? It actually gets warmer.
Sounds absurd, right? The secret is what a fridge really is.
Rub alcohol on your hand and it feels cool. As it dries, it carries heat away.
In the fridge's inner pipes, refrigerant evaporates the same way, pulling heat from inside.
The compressor squeezes that gas, making it even hotter.
The back coils dump that heat outside, and the refrigerant turns liquid again.
A narrow passage drops its pressure, so it turns cold and flows back inside.
This loop never stops pumping heat outward.
A fridge doesn't make cold. It moves heat.
So with the door open, it pulls heat out of your room and dumps it right back in.
Add the motor's heat, and the room actually gets warmer.
That's also why the back of your fridge is always warm.
