On-screen title (fixed for the whole video)
How does a fridge get cold?

Description
A refrigerator doesn't create cold air. It moves heat from inside to outside.
Refrigerant evaporates in the inner pipes and absorbs heat, gets squeezed hot by the compressor, dumps that heat through the coils at the back, and comes back cold.
That's why leaving the fridge open in summer actually warms the room.

Final English narration
A fridge doesn't make cold air. Sounds absurd, right?
So how does the inside get cold?
Rub alcohol on your hand and it feels cool. As it dries, it carries heat away.
In the fridge's inner pipes, a refrigerant does the same, evaporating and pulling heat from inside.
That heat-filled gas gets squeezed by the compressor and turns even hotter.
In the coils at the back, it dumps that heat and becomes a liquid again.
Through a narrow passage its pressure drops, and it turns cold and heads back inside.
This loop keeps pumping heat from inside to outside.
So a fridge doesn't make cold. It moves heat.
That's why an open fridge won't cool your room in summer.
The heat comes out the back, plus the motor's heat, so the room gets warmer.
