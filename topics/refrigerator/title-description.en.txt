Description
Leave the fridge open to cool your room? It actually gets warmer.
A refrigerator doesn't create cold air. It moves heat from inside to outside.
Refrigerant evaporates in the inner pipes and absorbs heat, gets squeezed hot by the compressor, dumps that heat through the coils at the back, and comes back cold. That's also why the back of your fridge is always warm.

Final English narration
Leave the fridge open to cool your room? It actually gets warmer.
Sounds absurd, right? The secret is what a fridge really is.
Alcohol cools your skin. As it dries, it takes heat.
Inside, refrigerant evaporates the same way, taking heat.
The compressor squeezes the gas even hotter.
The back coils dump that heat, and it turns liquid again.
A narrow passage drops the pressure, so it goes cold and flows back.
This loop never stops pumping heat outward.
A fridge doesn't make cold. It moves heat.
So with the door open, it pulls heat out of your room and dumps it right back in.
Add the motor's heat, and the room actually gets warmer.
That's also why the back of your fridge is always warm.
